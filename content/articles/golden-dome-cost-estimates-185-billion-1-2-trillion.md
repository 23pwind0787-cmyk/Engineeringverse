A forwarded video puts the question plainly: the Pentagon says Golden Dome costs $185 billion; Congress's analysts say $1.2 trillion. Both figures are real and correctly attributed — the gap exists because they price different systems over different periods.

## Where the numbers come from

Golden Dome began with Executive Order 14186, "The Iron Dome for America", on 27 January 2025, renamed "Golden Dome for America" soon after. The order expanded homeland missile defence to peer and near-peer adversaries — Russia and China, not just rogue states — and directed a reference architecture within 60 days. That architecture was never made public; it remains classified.

- **The original figure was $175 billion.** On 20 May 2025, President Trump announced the system would cost about $175 billion and be operational before the end of his term.
- **$185 billion is the Pentagon's update.** On 17 March 2026, programme director Gen. Michael Guetlein (US Space Force, head of the Office of Golden Dome for America, Senate-confirmed in July 2025) raised the estimate to $185 billion for the "objective architecture" through about 2035, adding $10 billion for three space systems. Lockheed Martin, RTX and Northrop Grumman have joined as prime contractors. Guetlein rejects the trillion-dollar estimates: "They're not estimating what I'm building."
- **$1.2 trillion is CBO pricing its own design.** The Congressional Budget Office's 12 May 2026 report, "Potential Costs of a National Missile Defense System", put development, deployment and operation at about $1.2 trillion over 20 years — but for CBO's own notional architecture built to match the executive order, not the Pentagon's actual design, which CBO says cannot be estimated from public information. In CBO's model, a 7,800-satellite space-based interceptor constellation alone accounts for $743 billion. An earlier CBO letter (5 May 2025) had put the space-based interceptor layer alone at $161–542 billion over 20 years. For wider context, the American Enterprise Institute's Todd Harrison put six possible architectures at $252 billion to $3.6 trillion over 20 years.

## What it is meant to stop, and how

The executive order's threat list covers ballistic, hypersonic and advanced cruise missiles plus "other advanced aerial attacks" — drones fall under that last category. The planned architecture layers proliferated space-based interceptors for boost-phase intercept, the HBTSS space tracking layer and the Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture custody layer, a ground and sea underlayer integrating existing GMD, Aegis, THAAD and Patriot systems, non-kinetic capabilities, and a unified battle-management network. CBO also warned that even its $1.2 trillion system could be overwhelmed by a full-scale Russian or Chinese attack.

## Status: funded, pre-operational, slipping

Congress has provided $24.4 billion through the FY2025 reconciliation law (P.L. 119-21), including $5.6 billion for space-based and boost-phase interceptors and $7.2 billion for space sensors. Space-based interceptor prototype and development agreements were awarded in late 2025–2026 — but no interceptor has flown and no architecture has been published. The timeline has slipped: initial capability is still targeted for summer 2028, integrating mostly existing systems, while the full objective architecture is now expected around 2035 rather than within Trump's term. The budget fight continues: the administration's FY2027 request is about $17.5–17.9 billion, while House appropriators' November 2026 draft tags about $9.4 billion and demands an independent CAPE cost estimate within 90 days. Industry executives, meanwhile, are publicly cautious about whether $185 billion covers the mission.
