The headline figure is real, and it is staggering: data centres and other large loads have requested about 474 gigawatts of power in Texas — more than five times the grid's all-time peak demand. The fuller picture, from ERCOT's own queue data, is that almost none of it exists yet, and Texas has hit pause to work out how much of it ever will.

## The 474 GW, precisely

ERCOT counted about 474 GW of large-load (75 MW and above) interconnection requests by June 2026 — up from about 63 GW in December 2024, 226 GW in November 2025 and 410 GW in March 2026. Governor Greg Abbott cited the 474 GW figure in his 3 August 2026 directive letter to the PUCT and ERCOT. One correction to the common framing: the 474 GW covers all large loads, not data centres alone — per Abbott's letter, about 90% of the requests are data centres, with crypto mining and other large industrial load making up the rest.

The "five times peak" comparison also checks out. ERCOT's long-standing record peak was 85,508 MW, set on 10 August 2023; a new preliminary record of 91,134 MW was set on 22 July 2026. Either way, 474 GW is roughly 5.2 to 5.5 times the biggest load the Texas grid has ever actually served.

## Requests are not demand

ERCOT itself cautions against planning on the raw total. Many requests are duplicates — one developer shopping the same campus to several utilities, or several developers chasing the same parcel. About 71.5% of the queue, roughly 294 GW, had no study submitted at all. ERCOT's 2025 load-forecast work found that sites historically reached only about 49.8% of their requested megawatts. Senate Bill 6 (2025) now forces applicants to disclose duplicate requests elsewhere in Texas and to post $50,000 per MW in financial security.

What is actually moving is far smaller: about 194 GW was conditionally included in ERCOT's "Batch Zero" study (September 2026); only about 9.5 GW is approved to energise; and observed peak use from those loads was only about 4.1 GW in September 2026.

## The pause

The slowdown in the forwarded video is confirmed — and stronger than the video suggests. On 3 August 2026, Abbott ordered a comprehensive PUCT and ERCOT audit of every data centre in the queue — about 1,800 projects halted — and ERCOT paused approvals to energise data-centre and crypto loads of 75 MW or more pending verification, with 17 loads (about 6.6 GW) stuck at that final gate. Ratepayer-protection rules bar passing data-centre infrastructure costs to households, and water use is an explicit audit concern. ERCOT's verification report is due to the PUC on 10 December 2026.

So: 474 GW requested, about 9.5 GW approved, about 4 GW actually in use. The gap between those three numbers — and what Texas decides to do about it — is the real story.
