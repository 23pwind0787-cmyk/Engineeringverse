The seeker is the part of a missile interceptor that actually finds the target. Leonardo DRS has been awarded a contract to develop a next-generation infrared sensor for the THAAD interceptor's seeker, announced on 19 November 2026 — with two details the short version of the story tends to miss: who awarded the contract, and what exactly is being built.

## An infrared focal plane array, awarded by BAE Systems

DRS is developing a next-generation infrared focal plane array (IR FPA) — the sensing element at the heart of the THAAD seeker — intended to enhance the seeker's ability to address "evolving and increasingly complex threats". The contract came from BAE Systems, which supplies the THAAD seeker, so this is a subcontract under that programme, not a direct Missile Defense Agency award.

No contract value was disclosed — Dow Jones reported that "terms of the deal weren't disclosed" — and the release gives no milestones or delivery dates. No dollar figure should be attached to it.

Development work is already underway at DRS's 200,000-square-foot foundry in Dallas, and the company is expanding capacity there to support the programme objective of increasing seeker production to about four times historical levels. Jerry Hathaway, senior vice president and general manager of DRS Electro-Optical and Infrared Systems, said: "THAAD is an important strategic program for our company, and we are proud to support this vital missile defense mission."

## THAAD, briefly

THAAD — Terminal High Altitude Area Defense — is a transportable, rapidly deployable, ground-based system that intercepts short-, medium- and intermediate-range ballistic missiles in their terminal phase, both inside and outside the Earth's atmosphere, destroying them by hit-to-kill kinetic impact. It is a critical element of the US missile defence architecture — and its effectiveness rests on exactly the kind of sensor DRS is now tasked with improving.
