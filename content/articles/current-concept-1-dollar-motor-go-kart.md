Give me a lever long enough, Archimedes said, and I can move the Earth. YouTuber Current Concept took the same idea and applied it to gearing instead of levers: if a small enough motor can be geared down far enough, can it move a go-kart with an adult aboard? The answer, demonstrated in his video "I built a go-kart with a $1 motor" (1 November 2026, over 2.3 million views within a week), is yes — at a pace, as he puts it, "that would impress even a snail".

## The motor and the kart

The motor is a tiny brushed DC motor costing $1 and weighing just 7 grams — about a quarter of an ounce. The kart is a rough secondhand machine bought through Craigslist for roughly the same $1 price as the motor, complete with unusual modifications that had to be undone and brakes that were removed entirely.

His calculation called for a 1,143:1 gear reduction. A single spur-gear pair at that ratio would need a driven gear roughly 95 feet across, so instead he printed a compound gearbox — each stage trading about 6× speed for about 6× torque — and the built gearbox came out at 1,243:1. That is the figure to quote: 1,143:1 is the calculated requirement, not the built ratio. At 1,243:1, the motor must rotate 1,243 times for a single revolution of the output.

## One motor, then fourteen

With a single motor, the kart moved with an adult aboard — slowly, but genuinely under its own power. Then he scaled up: 14 of the $1 motors mounted in a white printed plate, all feeding the same gearbox, wired to a LiPo battery and switched on together by a dashboard toggle. The gas pedal is not connected to anything.

The 14-motor version was faster — it cruised down the driveway — but it overheated almost immediately and ran only briefly. No top speed was ever given for either version, so none should be stated.

## A demo, not a vehicle

This is a working physics experiment, not a practical vehicle and not a joke build: it genuinely drives, just very slowly, and the point is the torque-versus-speed trade-off made visible. The creator suggests brushless DC (BLDC) motors could be worth testing in a future version. For now, the $1 motor's legacy is a go-kart that proved Archimedes right — and proved that torque alone does not make a fast kart.
