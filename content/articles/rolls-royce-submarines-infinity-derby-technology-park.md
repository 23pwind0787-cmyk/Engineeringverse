## The partnership

Rolls-Royce Submarines has announced a strategic partnership with INFINITY, announced on 6 November 2026, to develop large-scale prototypes of advanced defence components. INFINITY is creating a technology park in Derby that brings full-scale prototyping, digital engineering, testing and validation, skills development and supply-chain collaboration together on one site.

The Rolls-Royce Nuclear Skills Academy already operates on the site. Rolls-Royce Submarines, which employs more than 5,500 people, will now work with INFINITY to develop additional capabilities across the 200-acre park, letting it focus on delivery at its Raynesway site in Derby while developing new manufacturing techniques at purpose-built facilities.

## Three manufacturing technologies

The partnership will build sovereign capacity in three processes:

- **Hot isostatic pressing (HIP)** — applying high temperature and pressure to densify parts and remove internal defects.
- **Power beam welding** — high-energy beam welding for thick, high-integrity joints.
- **Additive manufacturing** — industrial-scale 3D printing of complex components.

The aim is faster development, higher capacity and lower material use and cost per unit for components used in nuclear fission and fusion, defence and aerospace programmes. UK Fusion Energy, the organisation delivering the UK's first prototype fusion power plant, has also recently announced a strategic partnership with INFINITY.

## Jobs and scale

The project is estimated to create 17,500 skilled jobs, supporting the East Midlands economy. That figure is an estimate for the wider technology park project, not a count of jobs already created — and the partnership itself is a capability-building agreement, not a production contract for specific submarine components. Neither Rolls-Royce nor INFINITY has disclosed the financial value of their agreement. Trade coverage of the wider project reports that construction of the first phase is targeted for 2027, with the first manufacturing capabilities expected from 2028.

## What the partners said

Lee Warren, Engineering & Technology Director at Rolls-Royce Submarines, said the INFINITY technology park lets the company focus on delivery at Raynesway "while continuing to lead the way in advanced manufacturing techniques for the next generation of nuclear power from purpose-built facilities."

Professor Gary Jones, chief executive of INFINITY, said a key objective of INFINITY is to support sovereign manufacture of advanced components, and that the agreement helps achieve that "by providing new capabilities for the UK Defence programme."

## Why it matters for engineers

For industrial and manufacturing engineering students, the three technologies at the centre of this deal — HIP, power beam welding and additive manufacturing — are exactly the processes that decide whether a large, high-integrity component can be made at all, and at what cost. A single site combining prototyping, testing, digital engineering and skills training is also a model worth watching: it shortens the distance between designing a component and proving it can be manufactured.
