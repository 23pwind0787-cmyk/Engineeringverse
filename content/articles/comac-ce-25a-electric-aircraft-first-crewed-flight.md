COMAC's first electric commercial aircraft has flown with a crew. The CE-25A, a 19-seat battery-powered aircraft, completed its first piloted flight on Friday 14 November 2026 at Binzhou Dagao Airport in Shandong Province, with two pilots and a flight test engineer aboard.

The flight itself was short, and the numbers deserve precision. The whole test lasted 57 minutes 46 seconds, but only 16 minutes 38 seconds of that was airborne, covering 60.4 km (37.5 miles). COMAC's stated design range is 400 km (249 miles) at a cruise speed of 300 km/h (186 mph) and a cruise altitude of 2,400 m — but that range is a design figure, not something demonstrated on this flight.

## A different kind of electric aircraft

The CE-25A is a 10-tonne-class aircraft (maximum takeoff weight 10 tonnes) developed by COMAC, led by its Beijing Civil Aircraft Technology Research Center, jointly with battery maker CATL, Shanghai Jiao Tong University and other domestic partners. COMAC and CATL set up a joint venture, COMAC Times (Shanghai) Aviation, in July 2023 to develop the aircraft's power system.

Its propulsion is unusual: ten electrically driven ducted fans integrated into the wing, five per wing, rather than a small number of large propellers. The aircraft has a T-tail and carries the registration B-0EFH. The designation itself is COMAC's explanation: C for COMAC, E for Electric, 25 for the CCAR-25 transport-category regulations it is being developed under, and A as the first product.

The programme has moved quickly by certification standards. The concept was first shown as a model at the China International Import Expo in Shanghai in November 2023. The first uncrewed autonomous flight took place on 2 February 2026, followed by 19 unmanned autonomous flight-verification tests before the crewed flight.

## Certification has only just begun

COMAC says the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC) has accepted its type certification application, formally starting the airworthiness review under China's CCAR-25 regulations. That is a milestone, but acceptance is not certification: no timetable for certification or entry into service has been announced, and the aircraft cannot carry paying passengers yet. The acceptance itself is reported via COMAC and Xinhua; no CAAC statement of its own has been seen.

What is not yet public is almost as important as what is. Motor specifications, the electrical architecture and the battery configuration have not been fully disclosed, and public sources do not confirm whether the flight used CATL's condensed-matter battery (500 Wh/kg at single-cell level), which Chinese industry reports have linked to the programme. Until those details are published, the CE-25A is best understood as a flying demonstrator of a new propulsion layout, not a near-service airliner.
