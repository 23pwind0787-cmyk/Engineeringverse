A Canadian armoured vehicle built on a pickup-truck chassis is now one of the most widely supplied armoured vehicles in Ukraine. The Roshel Senator, built by Roshel of Brampton, Ontario, has been delivered in numbers approaching 3,000 — and the reason is the chassis it sits on.

The Senator is not, strictly, a pickup with armour bolted on. It is a purpose-built armoured body mounted on a commercial Ford F-550 heavy-truck chassis (Ram 5500 and Toyota Land Cruiser 70 chassis are used for some variants). That commercial base is the whole point: a mass-produced truck chassis is faster and cheaper to build on than a purpose-built military platform, and it can be serviced with commercial parts. Roshel, founded in 2016 by Roman Shimonov, began production in April 2018 for law enforcement, SWAT and border patrol use; its first combat use was Ukraine in 2022.

## Protection and performance

The numbers are those of a serious infantry mobility vehicle, not a converted truck. The 6.7-litre Power Stroke diesel V8 makes 330 hp with a 10-speed automatic, in a vehicle of about 8 tonnes. The APC variant carries a crew of 2 plus up to 10 passengers (up to 12 in some configurations), with a payload of 1.2–2 tonnes; the MRAP variant carries up to 10 with a 1.5-tonne payload. Each vehicle costs about US $600,000.

Protection differs by variant. The APC is rated to CEN B7 ballistic level, withstanding 7.62 mm and .50-calibre fire at 30 m. The MRAP adds a V-shaped (double-V) hull, mine-protected seats, run-flat tyres and high ground clearance, rated to STANAG 4569 Level 2 ballistic and Level 3 blast — Roshel claims resistance to an 8 kg TNT mine under the wheel or centre, a 25 kg side blast and 155 mm fragmentation. Variants include the APC, MRAP, Emergency Response Vehicle, EOD and Pickup versions, plus a Ram-based MRAP; a new-generation Senator MRAP and a lighter "Captain" on a Ford Ranger Super Duty chassis were unveiled at Eurosatory 2026, built from data on more than 2,500 vehicles' combat experience.

## Nearly 3,000 — and who paid for them

Deliveries climbed steeply: 8 vehicles in May 2022 (Canada's first purchase), the 1,000th in December 2023, 1,400 by autumn 2024, 1,700 by March 2025, more than 2,000 by December 2025, more than 2,500 by May 2026, and nearly 3,000 by late October 2026 — announced when a first-generation Senator MRAP went on permanent display at the Canadian War Museum (the museum itself put it at "more than 2,700"). About 700 were delivered between May 2025 and May 2026 alone. The "nearly 3,000" figure is Roshel's own, not an independently audited count.

One common misunderstanding is worth correcting: Canada did not pay for all of them. Canada's own funding covers 591 vehicles — 8 in 2022, 200 in January 2023 (CAD 90 million) and 383 in February 2026 (about CAD 200 million, including 100 medical evacuation, 82 command and 201 APC variants; delivered ahead of schedule by October 2026). By March 2025, about 90% of the 1,700 delivered had been financed by foreign governments including Ukraine, with about 10% donated by Canada. Czechia financed 70 early on, and the US delivered 150 Senator MRAPs to Ukraine's Interior Ministry in 2025.

Production runs at about 120 vehicles a month with around 500 workers, about two weeks of assembly per vehicle, and Roshel has set up assembly and localisation in Ukraine, signed a June 2026 memorandum with Daimler Truck, and runs a plant in Michigan (opened 2024) alongside four facilities in Ontario.

In service the Senator does troop transport, medical evacuation, patrol and reconnaissance, and border guard and police work, across the Armed Forces, Border Guard Service, National Police and State Special Transport Service. Open-source tracking by Oryx documented 176 destroyed, damaged or captured as of December 2025. It is not without critics: Defence Industry Europe notes reports of chassis damage on rough terrain and armour plate failures in Ukraine. But the production story is the real lesson — a commercial chassis, built at commercial speed, can put more protected vehicles into a war than a bespoke military design built at military speed.
