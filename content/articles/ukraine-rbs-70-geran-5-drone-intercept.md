Ukraine's air defenders have scored a kill that matters beyond the single drone. The 1027th Anti-Aircraft Missile and Artillery Regiment has shot down a Russian jet-powered Geran-5 drone over the Kyiv region using Sweden's RBS-70 system — the first time an RBS-70 has downed a Geran-5, and the first such kill in the Kyiv region. The General Staff announced it on 13 November 2026, releasing video of the intercept. The exact time and location were not disclosed, and the kill itself is a Ukrainian claim, supported by the released footage.

The "first" needs its qualifier. It was the first RBS-70 kill of a Geran-5 — not the first Geran-5 kill overall: in September 2026 the 1020th Regiment released footage of a Ukrainian-made STING interceptor drone destroying one. The RBS-70 crew commander, callsign "Vigo", put it precisely: "as far as I know, we're the first in Kyiv Oblast to down such a UAV with our weapon." The crew had already described the engagement in an interview with Ukrainian outlet LB.ua published on 11 November.

## A faster drone needs a different weapon

The Geran-5 is a departure from the propeller-driven Shahed/Geran-2, which cruises at 150–180 km/h. It is a jet-powered strike drone Russia began fielding in early 2026, with a conventional aircraft layout rather than the delta wing, a Chinese Telefly TF-TJ2000A turbojet, about 850 kg maximum takeoff weight, a roughly 90 kg warhead, about 950 km range and a ceiling near 6 km. Ukraine's Defence Intelligence (HUR) puts its cruise speed at 450–600 km/h, with a maximum near 600 km/h; Vigo said the drone he engaged was flying at more than 500 km/h (310 mph). Those are Ukrainian intelligence estimates, not independently verified measurements.

That speed is the problem. At 450–600 km/h the Geran-5 dives toward its target only a few minutes before impact, leaving air-defence crews very little time — and it flies too fast and high for the interceptor drones and mobile fire groups that catch slower Shaheds, whose interceptors top out at 250–300 km/h. HUR published a teardown of a shorter Geran-5 variant on 9 November 2026, finding 93 foreign-made electronic parts.

The RBS-70, built by Saab, is suited to exactly this target because of how it guides. It is laser beam-riding: the missile follows a laser corridor projected by the operator and carries no infrared seeker head, so it cannot be decoyed by flares and is resistant to jamming. The tripod-mounted system has a range of about 5,000–8,000 m (the NG version over 9 km) and an altitude up to about 5,000 m. Sweden donated RBS-70 launchers (plus Giraffe 75 radar) from early 2023, with Ukrainian training beginning in January 2023; in February 2025 Sweden announced a 1.2 billion SEK (about $113 million) air-defence package procuring the latest RBS-70 version and Tridon Mk2 guns as a donation for Ukraine. The system already has a combat record in Ukraine: a Ka-52 helicopter near Robotyne in August 2023, a Su-24 in December 2023, plus cruise missiles and loitering munitions.
