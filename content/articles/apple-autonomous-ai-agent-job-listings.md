Apple has announced no autonomous AI agent. But it has posted job listings describing one in unusual detail — and Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported them on Thursday 19 November 2026, with the listing text quoted verbatim by TechCrunch, MacRumors, 9to5Mac and others. The accurate framing is "Apple is hiring to build…", and the listings themselves are primary documentary evidence.

## What the listings say

The key posting — "Senior Engineering Manager, Agentic AI – Proactive", dated 17 November and since pulled — reads:

*"Imagine an AI agent that can use a computer the way a capable person does. It browses the web, works across apps, runs commands, writes and debugs code, and calls services, and it chooses the right tool for each step. It does all this privately and with the polish people expect from Apple."*

A second posting describes agents that "plan and carry out multi-step tasks across apps, browsers, command lines, code, and APIs, and they can coordinate specialized sub-agents along the way" — general-purpose computer-use agents. The role works across the Siri, UI Frameworks and Apple Foundation Models teams. Note the emphasis: browsers, command lines and code put the Mac and computer use at the centre at least as much as a phone assistant, so this should not be read as purely a Siri or iPhone feature.

## Timeline: reporter-sourced, not an Apple commitment

Bloomberg says Apple "has eyed introducing the service as soon as the end of next year" — the end of 2027 — while stressing that development "remains in the early stages". Context from the same reporting: new CEO John Ternus is pushing software chief Craig Federighi to move faster on AI; Bloomberg had reported earlier in November 2026 that Apple wants Siri to become more proactive and agent-like, possibly as soon as next year; and Apple is simultaneously making third-party Mac agents harder to run, with macOS 27.3 reportedly adding warnings that discourage granting full access to third-party agents. Shares of Meta and Anthropic slid in late trading after the Bloomberg report.

What Apple has officially announced remains Apple Intelligence and a more personal, context-aware Siri with in-app actions, announced at WWDC 2024 and delayed. This agent programme is separate, earlier-stage, and so far evidenced only by the job postings plus Bloomberg's sourcing. The forwarded claim — an assistant that could control apps, browse the web and complete multistep tasks — matches Apple's own posting language almost word for word. What it is not, yet, is an announced product.
