## Why this list

A final-year project has three distinct jobs: find the literature, write it up properly, and present it. No single tool does all three well, so this guide groups fourteen tools by the job they do best, and ends with a simple setup that covers the whole project. Where a tool has a student offer, it is noted — and where a claim comes with a condition, the condition is stated too. Offers change, so check the tool's own page before you pay for anything.

## All-in-one AI assistants

### ChatGPT

- **Link:** https://chatgpt.com
- **Best for students:** writing assignments, solving coding problems, brainstorming FYP ideas.
- The most versatile AI for daily coursework — drafting, explaining concepts, debugging code and structuring arguments. Treat its output as a first draft: check facts and citations yourself before they go into your report.

### Google Gemini

- **Link:** https://gemini.google.com
- **Best for students:** research, with a real student offer.
- Works directly with Gmail, Google Docs and Drive, and accepts PDF uploads of your study material. Students aged 18 and over can claim a paid Google AI plan free for 12 months — in Pakistan that is Google AI Plus — by verifying student status through SheerID. The offer must be claimed by 31 December 2026, and it turns into a paid plan after the year unless you cancel.

### Claude AI

- **Link:** https://claude.ai
- **Best for students:** thesis, long reports and FYP documentation.
- Handles long documents — full chapters and reports — and gives clear, detailed explanations. The best of the three general assistants for working through a long draft section by section.

## Research tools

These matter most for an FYP, because the literature review is where most projects are won or lost.

### Perplexity AI

- **Link:** https://www.perplexity.ai
- **Best for students:** finding sources for your literature review.
- Gives answers with real citations you can follow up and use in your proposal, instead of uncited text you have to verify from scratch.

### NotebookLM

- **Link:** https://notebooklm.google
- **Best for students:** studying past papers and research PDFs.
- Upload your reference papers, ask questions about them, and get summaries with citations back to your sources. It only answers from what you upload, which makes it useful for staying inside your own literature.

### Consensus

- **Link:** https://consensus.app
- **Best for students:** finding scientific evidence for your topic.
- An AI search engine over peer-reviewed literature — Consensus says its corpus covers over 200 million academic papers. Ask a research question in plain language and it returns synthesised answers with citations, plus a meter showing whether the studies it found agree or disagree.

### Research Rabbit

- **Link:** https://www.researchrabbit.ai
- **Best for students:** literature review and finding related work.
- Shows visual maps of how papers connect to each other, so you can trace a topic backwards and forwards and avoid missing key references.

## Writing and proposal tools

### Grammarly

- **Link:** https://www.grammarly.com
- **Best for students:** fixing grammar in your FYP proposal and final report.
- Catches grammar, clarity and tone issues so your English reads professionally to a supervisor. The free tier covers the basics.

### QuillBot

- **Link:** https://quillbot.com
- **Best for students:** paraphrasing and rewriting sentences.
- Rewrites sentences from papers in your own words. One rule: paraphrasing is not a substitute for citing. If an idea came from a paper, cite the paper, however you phrase it.

### Hemingway Editor

- **Link:** https://hemingwayapp.com
- **Best for students:** making complex technical writing simple.
- Highlights hard sentences, passive voice and dense passages so your examiner can follow your argument easily.

## Presentation tools

### Canva

- **Link:** https://www.canva.com
- **Best for students:** FYP defence slides and project posters.
- Free templates and drag-and-drop design. For a defence deck or a poster, it produces cleaner results faster than building from a blank PowerPoint slide.

### Gamma

- **Link:** https://gamma.app
- **Best for students:** generating a first slide deck from your report.
- Paste your FYP text and get a structured presentation draft in minutes, which you then edit into your defence slides. Treat it as a starting point, not a finished deck.

## Notes and productivity

### Notion

- **Link:** https://www.notion.so
- **Best for students:** organising your FYP timeline, notes, tasks and references in one place.
- Notion's Plus plan is free for students and educators who sign up with a school email (one-member workspace). Note the limit: full Notion AI is not included in the free student plan — AI stays at a trial allowance — so use Notion for organisation and do your AI drafting in one of the tools above.

## Best simple setup for FYP students

- **Research phase** — Perplexity + NotebookLM + Research Rabbit.
- **Writing phase** — Claude for the draft, then Grammarly and QuillBot for polish.
- **Presentation phase** — Gamma for the slide draft, Canva for the poster and final slides.

Start with the free tiers and the student offers. The two offers with a deadline are Gemini's 12-month student plan (claim by 31 December 2026) and Notion's free Plus plan for students. Everything else on this list has a usable free tier.
