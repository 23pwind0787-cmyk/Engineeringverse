Oak Ridge National Laboratory has a new supercomputer in the world's top 15 — and it is built for a different kind of mission than its famous neighbour Frontier. Lux debuted at No. 14 on the 68th edition of the TOP500 list, announced 16 November 2026 at the SC26 conference in Chicago, with a measured performance of 220.79 petaflops on the High Performance LINPACK benchmark — 220 quadrillion calculations per second. That figure is a traditional simulation benchmark, not a measure of AI-training throughput. Lux opened to scientific users the same day, through the Oak Ridge Leadership Computing Facility (OLCF).

## The first AI factory for the Genesis Mission

Lux is the first dedicated US AI factory for the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission, in AMD's wording — ORNL calls it "the first major addition to DOE's computing infrastructure in support of the Genesis Mission". The Genesis Mission is a DOE-led initiative to transform science in the United States by uniting government, industry, academia and philanthropy, combining AI, supercomputing, quantum systems and advanced instruments. One terminology note: ORNL now calls it "Super Intelligence (SI), formerly known as artificial intelligence" — SI is AI in DOE's current usage.

The machine is a public–private partnership between DOE, AMD and HPE. It is built on the HPE ProLiant Compute XD685 platform with AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs, AMD EPYC CPUs, AMD Pensando networking and AMD ROCm software, and it is direct liquid-cooled. Around half of Lux's annual 3.5 million node-hours will support the Genesis Mission; the other half is available to AMD.

## Three first projects

Lux's first scientific projects are exactly the three named in the launch announcements:

1. **AI-assisted fusion power plant design** — an AI platform integrating high-performance computing tools and robotic system design.
2. **AI-accelerated materials development and advanced manufacturing** — an AI-based "foundation model factory" creating custom foundation AI models for US industry.
3. **AI-driven Earth system prediction** — forecasting water availability, streamflow and river temperature weeks to years ahead, supporting hydropower and thermoelectric operations in the Tennessee Valley Authority region.

Lux sits at the OLCF alongside Frontier, the world's fastest supercomputer for open science and the first exascale machine. A successor system, Discovery, is set for delivery in 2028. For now, Lux's job is to prove that a supercomputer built as an AI factory can turn the Genesis Mission's ambitions — fusion plants, new materials, and long-range Earth prediction — into working science.
