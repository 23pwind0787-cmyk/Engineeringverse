Algeria's People's National Army has released footage of a coastal-defence missile firing at a sea target off its western coast. The firing took place on 10 November 2026 at the Mers El Kébir naval base near Oran, during the tactical exercise "Mediterranean Falcon 2026" (Sakr El-Moutawassit 2026), part of the army's 2026–2027 combat preparation programme. The exercise was supervised by General Saïd Chanegriha, Minister Delegate to the Minister of National Defence and Chief of Staff of the People's National Army. According to the Ministry of National Defence, the target was destroyed "with great precision" — that is the ministry's claim, and no independent confirmation of the hit exists.

What makes the footage notable is the weapon in it. The ministry did not name the missile, but analysts identify it as China's CM-302 supersonic anti-ship missile, the export version of the YJ-12. The identification rests on the launcher visible in the footage — a wheeled vehicle with three rectangular canisters on an elevated coastal position, a layout consistent with the Chinese coastal-defence system. That is strong circumstantial evidence, but it is still inference: the article keeps "appears to be" because Algeria has not officially confirmed it.

## What the CM-302 is

The CM-302 is built by China's CASIC and is the export variant of the YJ-12, first marketed at the Zhuhai air show in 2016. Its advertised figures are a range of about 290 km (180 miles) — CASIC's own figure is slightly lower, at 280 km (170 miles) — a warhead of more than 250 kg, and sustained supersonic cruise of approximately Mach 2.5–3. The domestic YJ-12 is reported to reach up to Mach 4, so the Mach 2–3 figures belong to the export version specifically. No speed was measured or stated for this firing.

A few labels need care. "Carrier Killer" is a marketing nickname, not an official designation — CASIC marketed the missile as able to strike large warships including aircraft carriers and destroyers. And despite some online commentary calling it hypersonic, it is not: Mach 2.5–3 is supersonic. Nor should the longer 400–500 km ranges sometimes quoted be used here; those apply to domestic PLA variants of the YJ-12, while the export CM-302 is deliberately kept just under the 300 km Missile Technology Control Regime threshold.

## Not a first reveal

This firing is the clearest public view of a known system, not the debut of a new one. The International Institute for Strategic Studies lists the CM-302 (also designated YJ-12E) among Algeria's coastal-defence missiles in both *The Military Balance 2024* and the 2026 edition. Reports trace the acquisition to an order placed in April 2020, with SIPRI recording deliveries in 2022 and the system reported inducted in 2024. The Mediterranean Falcon firing is therefore less a revelation than a demonstration: Algeria showing, on camera, a coastal-defence capability it has quietly held for several years.
