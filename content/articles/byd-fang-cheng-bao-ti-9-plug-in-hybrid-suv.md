BYD's premium sub-brand Fang Cheng Bao is preparing to launch its largest SUV yet. The Ti 9 — "Tai 9" in Chinese — is a full-size six-seat plug-in hybrid, and its headline numbers are striking: BYD claims the battery charges from 10% to 97% in nine minutes, and a combined range of 1,630 km (1,013 miles) under China's CLTC test cycle. The official launch event is set for 19 November 2026 at 7:00 pm Beijing time, ahead of the Guangzhou Auto Show.

A word of caution before the numbers: the range and charging figures are BYD's claims. No independent test has verified them yet, and BYD has not disclosed the peak charging power, the charger or infrastructure required, or the exact test conditions behind the nine-minute figure.

## A flagship for the Ti series

The Ti 9 is the flagship of Fang Cheng Bao's Ti series and the brand's largest SUV: 5,270 mm long with a 3,130 mm wheelbase, in a 2+2+2 six-seat layout. (The regulatory filing actually lists 5-, 6- and 7-seat variants; the launch model is the six-seater.) It is the first model on BYD's new DMS hybrid platform, with unibody construction — unlike the body-on-frame Bao 5 and Bao 8 — plus the sixth-generation DM-i hybrid system, DiSus-A body control and God's Eye B assisted driving.

Power comes from a 1.5-litre turbocharged engine and two permanent-magnet electric motors, one on each axle, each rated at 200 kW (268 hp) for a combined 400 kW (536 hp). One figure needs a footnote: BYD's newer material and Car News China put the engine at 115 kW (154 hp), but the MIIT regulatory filing for the same 1.5-litre turbo engine lists 110 kW (147–148 hp). The discrepancy is unresolved, so treat the 154 hp figure as BYD's latest claim rather than settled fact.

## The battery and the nine-minute claim

The Ti 9 carries a 66.48 kWh second-generation lithium iron phosphate (LFP) Blade Battery. BYD claims an electric-only range of 310 km (193 miles) under the WLTC cycle, a combined CLTC range of 1,630 km, and up to 1,385 km under the stricter WLTP cycle (about 861 miles). Minimum-charge fuel consumption is claimed at 6.4 litres per 100 km under WLTC.

The charging claim is the one that has drawn the most attention. Using BYD's Flash Charging, the company says the battery goes from 10% to 70% in five minutes and from 10% to 97% in nine minutes. BYD also claims the system keeps working in the cold: 20% to 97% in 12 minutes at −30 °C, roughly three minutes slower than in normal conditions. What BYD has not said is how much power the charger must deliver to hit those times, or what charging hardware owners will need — so the nine minutes is a claim about the car and its charging system together, not a promise that any fast charger will do it.

## Where things stand

Pre-registration opened on 29 September, and pre-orders opened on 9 November with a 3,000-yuan (about $447) deposit; early buyers are offered six months of free flash charging. Show cars are already in 21 dealerships across 12 Chinese cities, and a Ti 9 rolled off BYD's Xi'an production line on 9 November as the company's 19-millionth new-energy vehicle. The global debut was at the Chengdu Auto Show in August 2026.

No price has been announced yet. The brand positions the Ti 9 in the roughly 400,000-yuan class, with dealer guidance of 350,000–400,000 yuan (about $48,400–55,300). No export programme has been confirmed. The 19 November launch should settle the price — and, once independent tests begin, whether the nine-minute charge and 1,013-mile range hold up outside BYD's own testing.
