Tuesday 17 November 2026 was a bad day for big American tech systems — and it is worth separating the two biggest stories, because they were not the same incident.

## Apple's outage: an internal update, by Apple's own account

Reports on Downdetector began around 11:30 AM ET and spiked around 12:29 PM ET; Apple's System Status page showed the App Store and Apple Music outages starting around 11:45 AM ET, with AppleCare on Device failing about 30 minutes earlier. Most consumer services were resolved by early to mid-afternoon — the App Store was marked resolved at 12:00 PM PT — and Apple's status page showed all services available by Tuesday evening US time, with some developer services lagging. Depending on the service, the disruption ran roughly 4 to 8 hours.

The cause is Apple-confirmed, not speculation. An Apple spokesperson told Bloomberg, Engadget, USA Today and TechCrunch: *"Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning. We're working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible."* Apple explicitly ruled out a cyberattack and gave no further detail. Note the wording: "an internal update" — a change to Apple's own server-side systems, not a software update pushed to users' devices.

The services officially listed on Apple's status page were iCloud Account & Sign In, the App Store, Apple Music, AppleCare on Device and iCloud Mail, plus developer services (App Store Connect, developer accounts, Music/Podcasts Connect APIs). Users were unexpectedly signed out of their Apple Accounts and hit repeated verification prompts, and retail store systems were also reported affected. Users also reported being unable to download apps or activate new iPhones, and Apple Pay problems were widely reported — but Apple Pay and device activation were not in the officially listed set on the status page, so those rest on user reports rather than Apple's own list.

## NSANet: same day, different cause

The NSA's classified internal network — its actual name is NSANet, one word — also went down on Tuesday, first reported as a CNN exclusive and corroborated by Nextgov and Fox News. But it was a distinct incident with a distinct cause. The NSA's official statement called it "temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance", said mission-critical capabilities remained fully operational, and found no evidence of a cyberattack. DNI Jay Clayton said systems failed to come back up after the agency patched major PHP vulnerabilities — the "PHP2Shell" flaws disclosed on 21 October.

"Intermittent problems" understates it. Fort Meade went to "code yellow" on Tuesday morning, then "code orange" on Tuesday night — closed to most employees — then "code gray" on Wednesday, with staggered 10 AM arrivals to avoid simultaneous logins. Restoration was still incomplete on Wednesday 18 November, and the Georgia cryptologic center was also affected.

Two outages on one day, amid a wider run of US tech failures (Microsoft on 12 November, the FAA, the banks) — but a coincidence of timing, not a linked event.
