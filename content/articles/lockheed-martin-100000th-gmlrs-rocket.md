Lockheed Martin has delivered its 100,000th Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System (GMLRS) rocket and is now producing nearly 14,000 rockets a year — a production rate reached ahead of schedule. The company announced the milestones on 10 November 2026 at its manufacturing facility in Camden, Arkansas.

The announcement came with a second piece of news: a new solid rocket motor developed with General Dynamics Ordnance and Tactical Systems (GDOTS) has completed its first test. Continued testing is planned, with motor qualification targeted for 2027. If it succeeds, the motor would give the programme a third domestic source for a key component and add resilience to the supply chain.

## A precision rocket built at scale

GMLRS is a GPS-aided, inertial-guided surface-to-surface rocket. Its standard variants strike targets at ranges exceeding 70 kilometres and carry either a unitary warhead for point targets or an alternative warhead for area targets. The rockets are carried in launch pods — six rounds in a HIMARS pod, twelve across the two pods on an M270 launcher — so the same ammunition serves both systems.

Lockheed Martin is also developing an Extended-Range GMLRS, designed to reach about 150 kilometres with a larger motor and an updated control system while keeping compatibility with both launchers.

The scale of the manufacturing effort behind the weapon is considerable. Lockheed Martin says the GMLRS programme involves more than 200 suppliers across 35 US states, including more than 50 small businesses. More than 200 employees support GMLRS production at the Camden site, and the company is increasing the site's overall workforce by 50 percent to support several munitions programmes, including the Patriot PAC-3 MSE interceptor and the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM).

## Why the motor matters

The GDOTS collaboration targets a different part of the production challenge: securing a reliable supply of rocket motors. GDOTS already supplies GMLRS components including warheads, rocket motor cases and launch pod containers. The newly tested motor is an additional step toward expanding domestic solid rocket motor manufacturing.

The first test does not mean the motor is ready for delivered rockets. Further testing and qualification remain, and Lockheed Martin has not disclosed the test results, the planned capacity of the new motor line, or how much additional annual output it could support once qualified.

> "Today's production milestones reinforce how Lockheed Martin is delivering with urgency, investing in our supply chain and ensuring American forces have what they need, before they need it, to defend here and around the world." — Gaylia Campbell, vice president and general manager, Lockheed Martin Tactical Missiles

The company marked the milestones with a ceremony in Camden, where it is also investing in workforce development — including funding an industrial automation and robotics laboratory at the new Defense Manufacturing Center of Excellence at Southern Arkansas University Tech. Lockheed Martin says more than 40 graduating high school students joined its manufacturing workforce this year.
