## What arrived in September 2026

SOLIDWORKS Design R2026x FD04, released in September 2026, centres on LEO, the AI virtual companion in SOLIDWORKS. According to SOLIDWORKS, the update adds three capabilities: automatic quality checks for sketch errors, an assessment of how a design change affects assemblies, and faster generation of manufacturing drawings.

The quality check is the most practical of the three for day-to-day work. It checks models, assemblies, and drawings against a company-specific or industry-standard checklist, so errors in a sketch can be caught before a design is released rather than discovered afterwards. The assembly risk assessment shows where a changed component is used, including every assembly it appears in, so the downstream effect of a change is visible before committing to it. The drawing update lets LEO handle sheet size, dimensioning, and tolerancing, or apply the user's own standards across a bulk request, keeping documentation consistent.

## How 2026 got here

FD04 is the fourth functional delivery of the 2026 cycle. SOLIDWORKS delivers five functional delivery and service pack releases each calendar year, with the general availability release followed by FD01 through FD04.

The earlier 2026 deliveries built the same AI direction step by step:

- **FD01, February 2026:** installing multiple versions of SOLIDWORKS Design side by side, plus three beta AI features: drawing generation through prompts, AI-guided root-cause analysis for part model errors, and an assembly structure generator that starts from a prompt.
- **FD02, April 2026:** an Assembly Performance Evaluator in beta, which uses natural-language prompts to analyse assemblies and recommend performance improvements, and a Design Change Impact tool in beta for evaluating downstream dependencies.
- **FD03, July 2026:** background drawing updates so drawings regenerate without a manual rebuild, a Fastener Pattern Assistant that groups bolts, washers, and nuts into patterns automatically, and AI large assembly management through LEO, including simplifying heavy geometry and recommending a lightweight strategy in conversation.

Features marked beta in the official notes are still in testing, so students and teams should expect them to change before they settle into the standard workflow.

## Why it matters for students

For engineering students, the pattern across 2026 is more useful than any single feature: the repetitive parts of CAD work — drawing setup, error hunting after a change, and managing large assemblies — are increasingly handled through prompts and automatic checks, while the engineer still decides the design itself. The FD04 quality check is a good example to practise with: a checklist-driven check rewards clean sketches and consistent standards, which are exactly the habits that matter in assessed design work and in industry.

Students on older releases are not locked out of the ideas. Background drawing regeneration, fastener grouping, and checklist-based checking all describe workflows that can be practised manually in any recent version: keep sketches fully defined, check drawings against a standard before release, and review where a part is used before editing it.

## What to check before upgrading

FD releases for the browser-based and cloud solutions are delivered automatically, while desktop service packs follow the usual update route. Availability depends on the licence and product tier, so check the official What's New page and the release notes for the product in use before assuming a feature is included. Pricing was not stated in the official update notes used for this article, and no price is claimed here.
