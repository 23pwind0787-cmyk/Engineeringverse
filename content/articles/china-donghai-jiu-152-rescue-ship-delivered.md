China's first batch of five domestically designed medium-sized marine rescue vessels is now complete. The final ship, Donghai Jiu 152, officially entered service with the East China Sea Rescue Bureau of the Ministry of Transport on Friday 13 November 2026, ending a procurement programme of nearly seven years.

All five ships share the same design — a 4,000 kW class (total installed power) medium-sized rescue vessel drawn up by the CSSC Shanghai Merchant Ship Design & Research Institute and built by CSSC Huangpu Wenchong Shipbuilding. Donghai Jiu 152 is 69.5 metres long with a 14.6-metre beam, and like her sisters she is built for all-weather rescue work in offshore and island-reef waters.

## Built to hold position in a storm

The ship's headline feature is its propulsion and positioning. It uses azimuthing electric propulsion — thrusters that rotate a full 360 degrees — with two azimuthing main thrusters and two side thrusters, giving it the manoeuvrability to work close to a casualty in rough water. It also carries a Class 2 dynamic positioning system (DP-2), which holds the ship on station automatically; Chinese sources describe this as a dual dynamic positioning system. Sister ships of the class make about 15 knots, with a bollard pull of 55 tonnes.

The vessel is rated to withstand winds of Force 12 — 118 km/h and above — and carries a first-class external firefighting system. A three-point anchoring system lets it hold a stable position in shallow water under Sea State 3 to support diving operations. Its core equipment — generators, propellers, side thrusters and towing winches — was domestically developed.

Capacity is a big part of the design: the ship can carry up to 100 rescued people, alongside its own crew.

## Five ships, three seas

The five vessels are spread across China's three rescue bureaus. The prototype, Donghai Jiu 151, was delivered on 25 December 2019 to the East China Sea Rescue Bureau. Nanhai Jiu 151 joined the South China Sea Rescue Bureau on 28 May 2026 and operates mainly in the Qiongzhou Strait off Hainan; Nanhai Jiu 152 followed in 2026. Beihai Jiu 151 was commissioned on 21 September 2026 for the North Sea Rescue Bureau at Yantai, Shandong — the first of the class in northern waters, with enhanced ice capability. Donghai Jiu 152 now closes the batch, returning the East China Sea bureau to two vessels of the class.

Together, the five ships give China's rescue service a standardised, domestically built fleet for the coastal and offshore waters where most of its rescue work happens — a capability that, until this class, relied on a mix of older and imported designs.
