On Monday 16 November 2026, customers of JPMorgan Chase — the largest bank in the United States — found transactions taking longer than usual, balances that would not load, and payments that would not go through. Reports began climbing shortly after 9 AM ET, and by the afternoon Downdetector had logged more than 3,300 user reports of problems with Chase, peaking near 4,800–5,000 around 2 PM ET before falling to about 1,780 by 4:45 PM. Chase said transactions were processing normally again by about 6:30 PM ET the same day.

## What Chase said

Chase's own statement, posted on X on 16 November, was precise about the cause: *"JPMorganChase has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems – there is no evidence this is cyber-related. It is taking longer than usual to service some customers."* Spokesman Joe Evangelisti told the Associated Press the same — it was "not a cyberattack" and "not a systemic issue" — and an FBI official told FOX Business there was no indication of external cyber activity.

Chase never said which systems failed, how many customers were affected, or exactly what went wrong. One claim in some secondary coverage — that a software update introduced the bug — comes from an FBI official via FOX Business, not from Chase, and should not be presented as the bank's own explanation.

## What customers experienced

The failures, as reported by customers, were login failures, inability to check balances, and delayed transfers, wires and Zelle payments. Downdetector's breakdown of Chase reports put about 53% down to balance and transaction issues, about 20% to login problems and about 17% to transfers and wires. Two framings matter here: the "thousands" figure is a count of user-submitted reports on Downdetector, not a Chase-confirmed number of affected customers — and Chase's own statement was softer than the customer reports, saying customers "still have access to their accounts and funds" while some transactions were merely "taking longer than usual".

## Other banks were not down

Early reports framed the incident as several banks going down at once. The banks clarified that it was centred on Chase alone. Bank of America, Citigroup and Capital One said their own services were operating normally; US Bank said its systems were not directly affected; and Zelle's operator, Early Warning Services, said the Zelle network itself remained operational. Downdetector also showed around 2,000 reports for Bank of America and around 1,000 each for Wells Fargo and US Bank, but those banks denied any outage on their side.

As Evangelisti put it, other banks "may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us." In other words: the outage was Chase's, and everyone else felt it only through payments that had to pass through Chase.
