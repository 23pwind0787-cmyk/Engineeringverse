Shipping has flirted with nuclear power for decades, but no nuclear-powered LNG carrier has ever been built. Samsung Heavy Industries (SHI) and US reactor startup Tesser Atomics now intend to change that: on 19 November 2026 the two companies signed a Memorandum of Understanding for a demonstration project to build the first nuclear-powered LNG carrier, with the demonstration vessel expected to operate commercially between the United States and Europe.

The pact is exclusive on both sides: SHI is Tesser's exclusive shipbuilder partner, and Tesser is SHI's exclusive reactor partner. SHI handles ship design, integration and construction; Tesser supplies the reactor technology and leads regulatory licensing.

## The Triton reactor

The ship would be driven by Tesser's Triton, a compact fluoride-salt-cooled high-temperature reactor (FHR) designed for ships, using low-pressure molten-salt coolant and TRISO fuel — the fuel the US Department of Energy, which developed it over two decades, calls "the most robust nuclear fuel on earth". Tesser says the reactor can be refuelled online while running: "They refuel while they run, so owners can choose when they want to buy new fuel," as CEO Dr Per F. Peterson puts it. Peterson co-founded Tesser and also co-founded Kairos Power; the Triton adapts Kairos's KP-FHR technology to maritime use. SHI's side was fronted by Kyunghee Kim, EVP and Head of the Future Business Division.

The project grew out of Norway's NuProShip research programme and continues within SFI SAINT, the NTNU-led consortium backed by the Research Council of Norway, where Tesser is the technology partner for large cargo ships. Separately, SHI had already obtained Approval in Principle from ABS and the Liberian Registry in September 2025 for an MSR-powered LNG carrier concept.

## An MOU, not a ship — yet

The stage matters. The release's own pathway runs "from concept validation to demonstration and commercial replication", and the parties "intend to develop the ship concept and marine reactor application". Nothing has been built or licensed: no construction contract, no budget or contract value, and no delivery date were disclosed. A Tesser spokesperson told Splash247 the first vessel is targeted for the early 2030s — a target, not a commitment. "World's first nuclear-powered LNG carrier" is the companies' framing of the project's goal, not an accomplished fact.

Two figures circulating in some summaries — a 100 MWt reactor and a 174,000 m³ vessel — do not appear in the press release and should not be treated as announced specs. And the reactor's performance claims are company design claims for an unbuilt, unlicensed marine reactor. What is real is the partnership and the intent; the ship itself is years, and several regulatory hurdles, away.
