A firefighting robot's whole purpose is to go where firefighters cannot. Beijing Lesentech's RXR-M80D-AX2 makes that claim in extreme form: according to the manufacturer, the robot spent 30 minutes inside a furnace at 1,832 °F — exactly 1,000 °C — and remained operational afterwards, protected by what the company calls multiple patented thermal-protection technologies.

## What checks out

The machine is real, and every hardware figure in the coverage matches Lesentech's published specifications. (The maker is Beijing Lesentech, part of Beijing Lingtian Intelligent Equipment Group; "RXR-M80D" is a generic Chinese fire-robot platform designation sold by several makers, and the "-AX2" is Lesentech's high-temperature-resistant variant.)

- **Cannon:** water/foam, maximum flow 80 L/s, range 85 m, at 1.0 MPa working pressure, with jet and mist-spray modes; rotation 60° horizontal, −15° to +75° vertical.
- **Sensors:** gas sensors for carbon monoxide, methane, hydrogen sulfide and hydrogen, plus temperature monitoring; imaging from visible-light cameras and an infrared thermal camera.
- **The rest of the sheet:** 800 kg; 1,750 × 1,100 × 1,200 mm; IP65 enclosure; top speed 1.5 m/s; climbs 30° slopes; crosses 400 mm trenches and 170 mm obstacles; 4-hour endurance; wireless remote control to 1,000 m line-of-sight.

These are catalogue figures, consistent with sibling RXR-M80D spec sheets — not independently measured values.

## The furnace claim is the manufacturer's own

The 30-minute, 1,000 °C survival traces to a company-released demonstration video, embedded in the coverage that appeared on 19–20 November 2026. No independent test or third-party certification of the furnace run was found, and secondary reporting explicitly flags that the data comes from the manufacturer. The "operate in 1,000 °C fire interiors for more than 30 minutes without external cooling" line is also Lesentech's standing marketing claim for this robot line since its launch around 2020 (shown at CIPPE 2022) — the furnace video is a demonstration of that design rating, not a new certified test.

It is also worth being precise about what a furnace run proves: thermal endurance in a controlled furnace, not performance in a real fire, with its smoke, debris and uneven heat. None of that makes the robot unimpressive — the specs are serious, and heat-rated fire robots are a real and growing class of machine. But the honest label for the headline claim is "according to the manufacturer", and that is how it should travel.
